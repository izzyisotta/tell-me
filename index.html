recording · a favour, from Izzy

Tell me about it.

The thing I'm building needs to learn how people actually talk about their days. Voice-note me yours: a tap, then talk, whenever you've done a thing.

the ask

Five to ten voice notes over the next few weeks.

  • A thing is a meal, a pub, a film, a gig, a walk, a trip, a party, or a person you saw. Anything that happened. Tell me about it the way you'd tell a mate.
  • Say the specifics. Who was there, where you were, what you thought, whether you'd go back. Names are welcome, that's the whole point. Go easy on other people's secrets, though: if it's something they'd mind being written down by a stranger, leave it out.
  • Ramble. Thirty seconds is fine, three minutes is lovely. No polish, no structure, no do-overs. One is still a gift.

on iphone · one minute, once

Get the shortcut. Then it's one tap.

It's an iPhone shortcut called Debrief. It lives right here on this page, behind the word I sent you.

Unlock the shortcut

Type the word from my message. Lowercase, one word.

＋ Get the shortcut
    1. Add it. Unlock above, tap the green button, and when Shortcuts opens tap Add Shortcut. That's the install, done once.
    2. Put it somewhere you'll hit it. Action Button (Settings → Action Button → Shortcut → Debrief) if you have one, or in the Shortcuts app hold Debrief and tap Add to Home Screen.
    3. Tap. Talk. Tap to stop. It sends itself to me and keeps a copy in your own Files (iCloud Drive → Shortcuts → Debrief), so nothing is lost if you're on the Tube.

    Not on iPhone? Then it's even simpler: WhatsApp me the voice notes. You know where I am.
